## Why it exists

In the summer of 1956, at the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, John McCarthy wanted an algebraic list processing language for AI work on the IBM 704 computer ([McCarthy's history](http://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/lisp/node2.html)). The list language built on FORTRAN, called FLPL, had no conditional expressions and no recursion, so McCarthy designed his own; its implementation began at MIT in the fall of 1958 ([McCarthy's history](http://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/lisp/node3.html)). The team decided on garbage collection, a way for the runtime to find and free memory the program no longer uses, to manage Lisp's storage ([McCarthy's history](http://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/lisp/node3.html)). McCarthy's 1960 paper was the first journal publication about the language ([McCarthy's history](http://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/lisp/node5.html)).

## What it trades away

Lisp is a family, and the family has no single standard. Common Lisp's formal standard, X3.226-1994, is a standard for Common Lisp, not for the entire Lisp family ([CLHS help](https://www.lispworks.com/documentation/lw51/CLHS/Front/Help.htm)). Implementations such as SBCL add extras beyond that standard, such as their own garbage collection controls and Unix support, so code that uses them does not move cleanly to another system ([SBCL manual](https://www.sbcl.org/manual/)). Quicklisp, the library installer, says its software updates are usually available about once per month ([Quicklisp](https://www.quicklisp.org/beta/)). The free LispWorks Personal Edition exits after 5 hours in one session ([LispWorks](https://www.lispworks.com/downloads)).

## Where it won

ITA Software picked Allegro CL as the original development environment for its airfare shopping engine, and used Lisp for the high-level structure of the application ([Franz](https://franz.com/success/customer_apps/data_mining/itastory.lhtml)). Grammarly states that its core grammar engine is written in Common Lisp and processes more than a thousand sentences per second ([Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/engineering/running-lisp-in-production/)). Naughty Dog designed GOAL, a Lisp dialect, for the development of its games ([Franz](https://franz.com/success/customer_apps/animation_graphics/naughtydog.lhtml)). GNU Emacs, the text editor, has an interpreter for Emacs Lisp, a dialect of the Lisp programming language, at its core ([GNU Emacs](https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/)). Autodesk introduced AutoLISP, a Lisp-based interface, into AutoCAD in the mid-1980s ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2015/ENU/AutoCAD-AutoLISP/files/GUID-49AAEA0E-C422-48C4-87F0-52FCA491BF2C.htm)).
