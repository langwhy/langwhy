## Why it exists

In May 1959 a committee formed at the Pentagon to design a business language that was easy to read ([Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/tdih/may/28/)). COBOL was created as part of a US Department of Defense initiative for a language that could work across operating systems and hardware ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/cobol)). The consortium behind it, CODASYL, formed in 1959 ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/cobol)). Its designers hoped a program would run on any computer with a compiler, with only minimal modifications ([Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/1960/)). Grace Hopper's A-0 compiler work led to FLOW-MATIC, the first English-language business compiler, in 1957 ([Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/1957/)). Jean Sammet was a key member of the committee that developed COBOL from 1959 to 1961 ([Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/profile/jean-sammet/)).

## What it trades away

COBOL trades brevity for readability. Its English-like syntax is self-documenting, with an emphasis on verbosity and readability, so programs are wordy ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/cobol)). Old programs also do not move cleanly between versions. IBM lists standard changes that can potentially affect existing COBOL programs, such as letters that now count as formatting codes in the descriptions of data fields ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SS6SG3_6.5/lr/ref/rlstdim.html)). The GCC compiler gcobol follows the 2023 standard plus extra features for older code, and its authors note that a strict COBOL-85 compiler would not compile most existing code ([GCC](https://gcc.gnu.org/onlinedocs/gcc-16.1.0/gcobol/gcobol.html)). Finding people to maintain it is hard too: GAO reported in 2025 that relying on COBOL and assembly language carries risks, including rising costs to buy and run these systems ([GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107795/index.html)).

## Where it won

Banks and government agencies run COBOL on IBM's z/OS mainframe platform. The Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce (CIBC) upgraded 40,000 business-critical COBOL applications to IBM Enterprise COBOL for z/OS 6 ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/products/cobol-compiler-zos)). GAO's 2025 review of federal legacy systems lists a US Department of Agriculture personnel and payroll system that is 41 years old and uses COBOL, and it reports that two US Treasury systems in the review run on COBOL and assembly language ([GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107795/index.html)).
